# IQON product-copy research notes

Prepared 10 September 2026. Draft copy is in `product-copy.json`.

## Scope and implementation

- The mapping contains all 14 current preview IDs: 11 supplements and 3 skincare concepts. It preserves `descriptor`, `description`, `highlights`, `title`, `story`, `facts` and `faqs`.
- Six entries add an optional `education` object with `eyebrow`, `title`, `body`, `scope` and `sources` (each source has `label` and `url`).
- Render source links and the ingredient-research scope with each education block. The biological explanations in the adjacent story and FAQs refer to those same sources.
- Product facts come from the supplied IQON catalog and content files: names, formats, counts, net weights, flavors and grass-fed label descriptions. No IQON efficacy study, ingredient quantity, complete formula, purity, certification, sourcing location, serving size or use frequency was established.
- The three skincare entries remain expressly labeled concepts. Their sizes and routine positions come from the existing preview; no peptide, ceramide, cleansing-performance or skin-type claim was added.
- Keto-5, GLP-1 Support, Liver Support, Colon Gentle Cleanse and Hair, Skin & Nails have no confirmed active ingredient list. Their copy therefore stays with the known identity, practical format and the product-specific questions a customer would reasonably ask. More benefit-led copy requires the actual formulas.
- No adjacent research-use peptides were researched or introduced. No repository, checkout, pricing or live product content was changed.

## Ingredient sources

### Creatine

[Hultman et al., “Muscle creatine loading in men,” Journal of Applied Physiology (1996)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8828669/) is an original human study of creatine accumulation and decline in skeletal muscle. Used to establish the research topic and that muscle stores change over time. Its supplementation protocols are deliberately not reproduced as IQON directions.

[International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand (2017)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z) supports the background explanation of the creatine/phosphocreatine system, ATP and the prominence of monohydrate in the research literature. This is the society's own published position statement and literature synthesis, rather than an original trial. No safety or performance promise is attributed to IQON.

### Collagen

[Proksch et al., Skin Pharmacology and Physiology (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23949208/) reports a placebo-controlled trial of a specific collagen hydrolysate in 69 women over eight weeks. The education copy distinguishes the elasticity result from the nonsignificant moisture finding. The trial's specific preparation and results do not establish effects for either IQON collagen.

### Colostrum

[Marchbank et al., American Journal of Physiology—Gastrointestinal and Liver Physiology (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21148400/) is a small crossover study in 12 volunteers examining bovine colostrum and exercise-related intestinal permeability. Used only as a concrete example of the research question. It does not establish IQON's animal source, processing method, immune effects or general digestive benefit.

### NMN

[Yoshino et al., Science (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33888596/) is an original ten-week randomized trial in postmenopausal women with prediabetes who were overweight or obese. Used for NMN's relationship to NAD+ biosynthesis and a narrowly described metabolic study. It was not a lifespan trial or a study of this IQON product.

### Resveratrol

[Timmers et al., Cell Metabolism (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22055504/) reports an original crossover trial in men with obesity. [Yoshino et al., Cell Metabolism (2012)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23102619/) reports no improvement in metabolic function in nonobese women with normal glucose tolerance. They provide concrete contrasting examples for why population and study design matter. No product effect, longevity outcome, source or trans-resveratrol specification is inferred.

## Other background checks

The [NIDDK prescription weight-management medication overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/prescription-medications-treat-overweight-obesity) informed the GLP-1 Support FAQ's distinction between a supplement preview and prescription medication, and the suggestion to have a prescriber or pharmacist review a complete formula before combining it with medicine.

The [NCCIH overview of detoxes and cleanses](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know) was reviewed to avoid adding unsupported detox or cleansing benefits. No broad detox claim was added to the product copy.

## Source access notes

PubMed pages sometimes returned empty content with a trailing slash and full article metadata/abstracts without one. Readable records were obtained for the linked creatine, collagen, colostrum, NMN and resveratrol papers. Some full-text publisher and PMC pages were unavailable; do not imply every full paper was reviewed. The copy uses readable abstract-level findings and the accessible ISSN text. This is targeted content research, not a systematic review.

For production, any approved formula supplied later should replace pending details rather than layering more placeholder explanation onto the page.
